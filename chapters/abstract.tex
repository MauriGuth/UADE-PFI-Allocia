\chapter*{Abstract}
\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{Abstract}

Argentine retail investors usually hold stocks, CEDEARs, exchange-traded funds and crypto assets across several platforms. The tools they use show the performance of each account, but neither the composition of the whole portfolio nor the external context that affects it, and their alerts do not explain why they appear. This final engineering project presents Allocia, a multi-asset portfolio analysis platform with two clients, web and mobile, sharing one account and one API.

The system classifies positions with its own catalogue, computes weights, exposures by asset class, sector, region and currency and the Herfindahl--Hirschman concentration index, incorporates geopolitical context and emerging signals with source and date, and generates explainable observations through a deterministic rules engine that shows the rule, the metrics and the evidence behind each one. A separate layer writes per-asset reports with a language model restricted to public sources, with literally validated citations and abstention when evidence is missing. The system informs and explains; it neither executes trades nor recommends buying or selling.

User research combines a survey of 190 people and three transcribed interviews, which agree on the dispersion of information and on the demand for verifiable explanations. Development follows Scrum with a Kanban board, in two-week sprints. Evaluation comprises 62 service, integration, security and artificial-intelligence checks, 33 unit tests, 27 browser tests and verifications against the published service; calculations match their manual reconstruction. The report also analyses the legal framework (Argentine Laws 25,326 and 26,831 and CNV regulations), security and privacy, the freemium business model and its financial viability under three 36-month scenarios.

The results meet the general objective at its core: a portfolio loaded from either client is measured and produces observations whose grounds can be inspected. A comprehension study with five participants shows that active investors understand the observations and their evidence, while inexperienced profiles require simpler language; the resulting changes were implemented in both clients.

\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} portfolio analysis, diversification, geopolitical risk, explainable artificial intelligence, rules engine, crypto assets, retail investor.
